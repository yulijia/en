I just finished filling out this year's AP&D, and I must say this year's new system is better than the old one at my university.
At the same time, I found my note that when I attended the university's workshop about academic development, and I took a lot of notes on how to write academic development plan. 

So, for a postdoc or junior member at the university, if you want to keep going up in the academic system, there is something called an individual development plan or academic development plan. That is something you need to fill in each year to reflect what's your achievement and what's your next year (a few years) plan.

I still remember when I visited UAB,  my colleague who is a new postdoc he's supervisor is a medical doctor and very kindly introduced that science IDP system to him (although that seems not a compulsory component for contract-based postdocs based on my observation). I believe as a new incoming postdoc, if you superviosr doesn't introduce it to you, you might lost some chance to learn about the academic system, and how to organise your achievement and plan every year.

Here, I would like to share my notes on this.

- University doesn't know what they want (no simple directions).
- Help staff.
- Look at your peers' real needs.
- Identify people who support me in doing that.
- Get out and explain who is great, so that we can work together.
- Find a mentor.
- Continue teaching (in some systems, they require these components).

#### 1. AP&D
Create your own local AP&D, maybe track your progress every day. If someone asks you to do something but you think it will take time and also won't help you build your current IDP, say "Thank you. But my own interest is blablabla."

#### 2. Elevator pitch
If you meet some senior people, always say what you have done recently. That will help to create an impression that you are doing well in your work and they might want to build collaboration with you.

#### 3. Ask for your needs
Ask for your needs repeatedly.
- For example, you can ask to be relieved of repetitive tasks or work that does not contribute significantly to your career development.
- I really like what I am currently doing, but I don't have the capacity. Explain which activities you value and want to prioritise.
- It's simply, but not possible. Acknowledge that you cannot take on every responsibility because your capacity is limited.

#### 4. Trust your peers / seniors
Build trusting relationships with senior colleagues who can advocate for you when you are not in the room. Remember to reciprocate their support and help others in return.
- You can show your support, such as by helping nominate colleagues for awards, recognition or professional opportunities.

#### 5. Work hard to be visible
But also speak kindly.

#### 6. Promotion
- You can skip levels.
- Use an external offer to negotiate promotion.

#### 7. The sooner I start, the better
- Ask someone to review your application.

#### 8. Make a self-summary for the whole year's AP&D

#### 9. Talk to the Research Office to help you promote your research.

#### 10. Join the faculty research committee of your faculty and school.
- You might get a chance to see past years' grant applications.
- Become a grant reviewer.

#### 11. Give a guest lecture if possible.

#### 12. Send ICT staff a thank-you letter (CC his supervisor).

#### 13. Benchmark your achievements against other academics at the same level, potentially including international comparisons, to understand what is expected for promotion.

#### 14. Grants
Demonstrate multidisciplinary collaboration, particularly through joint grant applications and academic communities.

#### 15. Be a co-lecturer, join communities that would benefit you, and also mentor students.

#### 16. Identify gaps in AP&D goals.

#### 17. Highlight evidence needed for promotion in the AP&D system (attach supporting materials).
